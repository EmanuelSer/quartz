I died tonight in a dream. Although it would be more accurate to say it was a self-inflicted end, an act of self-erasure. 
There was a house party going on. However, for reasons unknown even to me, I got sick of it, as I found myself detached, like a bird perched on a distant branch, observing but not participating. Unseen and unnoticed, I decided to wander around the house where I stumbled upon an empty room.
Memories of a house from another life came to me, one I visited often in the innocence of childhood. Feeling my absence to be inconsequential to the rest, I came up with a lame excuse of needing to power my phone, grabbed a slice of pizza and a glass, and went off to my newfound solitude. I locked myself in the room, sealing me within its four walls. In a shadowy corner I sat in silence; the room's light remained off, but the party's glow slipped through a window, casting an ethereal light.
The window, hidden perhaps behind some furniture, seemed invisible to the outside world. On the inside there was a thick white curtain which muffled the chatter making it a comforting hum, their words indistinct and unimportant. I ate my pizza and sipped my drink, idly scrolling through TikTok. After a while I wrapped myself in a cotton sheet, went back to my corner, and surrendered to the inevitable.

I don't recall any pain, only an awareness of the other. I was there, I was myself, I was dying, and then I was no more. The end was strangely serene, a blissful conclusion to the narrative of "me".
Just then did I remember someone had actually noticed I was getting into the room; a voice, "What are you doing?" she had asked, a hint of concern in her words. "I just want to be by myself for a while," I'd replied. Her eyes revealed she didn't fully believe me. She had offered some resistance, even attempted to enter the room.

I wonder, would this memory haunt her? Would she be able to sleep afterwards without remembering the sight of my tear-filled eyes? 
Such is the weight of words left unsaid and actions left unexplained. In the end, all that is left for her is the echo of my final act, a memory that may linger much longer than I ever did.
