The fact that this film is an **adaptation from a book written by a monk** really ties every aspect of it together.

Upon first impression, its strangeness strikes the viewer like a couple of bright red dots floating over a dark background. However, this feeling quickly fades away as the movie draws you into its own reality. A reality that is nothing but our own, observed through the eyes of **Apichatpong Weerasethakul**, and this is in my opinion the key factor that makes this film an extraordinary experience.

Everything surrounding the production and distribution is almost as fascinating as the film itself. From the inaccessibility of the book it's based on, to the **art instalation** the director created around the concepts of **death and life**, where he used this film as the closing piece. All of this left me wishing for more once the film ended. A feeling similar to that of waking up after a very **detailed dream**. Like saying goodbye to a life you enjoyed for a while and the burden of having to re-adjust to your regular life. Slowly loading in those mundane things of daily life that tend to worry you.

This, however, is not exactly an escapist film. Despite how comforting it's atmosphere may be, this movie is facing our own reality. It's heaviest and hardest topic. Screaming at you, in such a law and tender voice, that **life is ephemeral**.

**Uncle Boonmee is dying**. Behind he leaves a life, and people he loved. But he is more concerned about how full he be able to join his **ghost wife** once he too passes away. He reflects upon his life and a few other scenes he lived before. Although it isn't cleare who he incarnates on this other scenes. Which makes you consider every character's point of new.

There are **ghosts** on this film as well as creatures that would normally inspire fear and terror but not here. Here they just exist among us so naturally that those who find it off putting are seen as the strange ones. This coupled with its take of what our **souls cling to once detached from their bodies**, really made me reevaluate my own **relationship with death**.

Perhaps I would find comfort on the **Buddhist view** on it. And that is another thing the film excels at: Exposing us to the world-shattering **cosmovisions** that exist around the world. Reminding us that all humans share a lot of experience but we are **free to react and live them differently**. With no right or wrong way to go about it.

Technically speaking there is also a lot to be said. About it's **cinematography** being breathtaking and yet so familiar, so down to earth. About it's **rhythm** and how it manages to **bend time** to make it slow and yet so short, just as life is.

But I don't think it would be faire to focus on such matters when the movie tackles such thought provoking themes.

In a way it reminded me heavily of **An Elephant Sitting Still**, but arriving to a very different conclusion.

Wish I could read the book this was based on.
