# Schoolgirl

I was certain I had already written about this book since it's actually the second time I've read it.
I'd like to point out that the first time around I found the book a little pretentious and a bit out of character. Thought the author had failed to portray the struggles of a true girl. For why most a man write of the struggles of a little girl?

Osamu Dazai’s Schoolgirl is a unique coming-of-age story that depicts the inner turmoil of a young girl in a way that is both honest and heartbreaking. The story follows the life of a young girl, Yae, as she struggles to find her place in the world and come to terms with her feelings of loneliness and inadequacy. Yae is a thoughtful and introspective character, and Dazai captures her innermost thoughts and feelings with remarkable clarity. From her struggles with her parents to her longing for companionship, Yae’s thoughts and feelings are portrayed in a way that is both sympathetic and emotionally charged. 

The novel is written in a simple yet effective style that captures the mood of the story perfectly. Dazai’s writing is evocative and vivid, and his descriptions of Yae’s inner life are particularly powerful. The book also touches upon themes of identity, alienation, and self-acceptance, and these themes are explored in a sensitive and thoughtful manner.

Overall, Schoolgirl is a powerful and moving novel that will stay with readers long after they have finished it. It is a unique and touching portrait of a young girl’s struggles and her journey but many would argue that this struggles should really be told from the perspective of a woman given that it is a woman's story.
The voice of a man telling this story makes it very hard to relate to and it somewhat takes away from the power of the story since it is a woman's story.

But now I have to disagree with myself as reading the rest of Dazai's novels paints a better picture of what the objective of this story was. 
Osamu Dazai may posses a lot more empathy that most other authors and of course I can't know for sure if the depiction of a teenage girl from 1939 in Japan was indeed inaccurate. Such a different context makes the judgment quite unfair, after all. But the fact is the character is portrayed wholly. The author doesn't even describe her appearance until halfway through the book, which cannot be said about other male authors writing female characters. And above all he manages to capture so much more than just a well rounded character. 

The book is written in such an intimate manner. Schoolgirl asks about identity and the value of individuality as the character ponders the possibility of being part of something bigger than her in an effort to give some purpose to her life. This is after all a human story, a story of loneliness and a story of the need to belong.
