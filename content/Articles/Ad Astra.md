A film by American film director **James Gray** released in **2019** is an impressive work of sci-fi.

The most interesting aspect about this film and the trait that the audience has valued the most it's the importance it gives to **character development and introspection**.

Ad Astra is a film about **humans in the search for humanism**. Told through the story of an astronaut and his relationship with his goal driven father. The irony is we are looking for it outside our planet in the vast and cold of space. In the end our main character realizes this and decides to look **inwards** and enjoy what human life has to offer.

There are a lot of rights in this film but they are so formulaic that when it decides to be a bit more conceptual and/or experimental it really sticks out and pulls the viewer out of the narrative if only for a split second.

If I could change anything from it I'd make it a bit more **subtle**. I realize this is more of a personal opinion but because the film's distribution plan required it to be a little less thought provoking. Or rather less challenging for the disperse minds. And it clearly worked based on its reception in theaters.

However, in my opinion, some of the **psychological sessions are unnecessary and lazy**. We as audience ARE capable of deciphering his emotions, based on context and his expressions. We can think and we want to.

I also believe the **voice over was added after the fact** because someone thought it was too confusing but it feels like we are being **fed in the mouth as babies**.

I did enjoy the film but that doesn't prevent me from criticising it as objectively as possible. The truth is the film was **dumbed down**. Probably by the studio but it really hurts a movie that would have otherwise been refreshingly challenging.

Also the **father should have been dead**. His mean lines at the end feel like a stretch. His character is bidimensional and that would have been fine if all we ever see of him are memories and recordings. But he is there in the flesh and still only has an objective. People aren't like that. We are just passionate. To death.
