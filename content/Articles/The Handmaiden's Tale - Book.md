# The Handmaiden's Tale

Definitively a book with a strong message and while I might not be the best person to make an interpretation on the subject, given my lack of knowledge on the topic and the indirect relationship I have with the issues brought up by the author, I want to take a moment to write about this book for the profound mark it left on me. 

The Handmaiden's Tale, written by Margaret Atwood, is a dystopian novel that follows the story of a woman in a repressive society. It speaks of how she is controlled, oppressed and subjected to the laws of her society. The book takes us through **her** journey, as she slowly discovers her true place in the world and finds her own strength to fight for her rights. 

I was deeply moved by the book and the message it conveyed. It made me think deeply about the current state of things and what needs to be done in order to fight for true equality. It also made me realize how much I have taken for granted - how I have been living in a society that has allowed me to have certain freedoms and liberties, which other women around the world are not allowed to have. The book also made me realize that there is a great need for more feminist literature to be brought to the forefront of our collective unconscious. It made me realize that there are many stories out there that need to be heard, and that most people would benefit from reading stories of women from all walks of life and from different cultures who are fighting for their rights. It made me realize that these stories need to be shared and that they need to be heard in order to truly understand the struggles and challenges that women face all over the world and for something to change.

A few important notes on the story that I believe help convey the message of how isolated and stripped of their humanity women are on a society that only sees value on their bodies. Which is barely an exaggeration from how our current society views women.

The decision to keep the narrator's name unknown, that alone makes you feel quite uneasy during the whole book. Always hoping that it'll be revealed. But this is not the story of a single individual, this was not a feel good story about a woman who defeats the odds against her and breaks free. 

The feeling that the most important battle is taking place inside the mind of our protagonist is one that I will never forget. Every time I think back on this book I remember that extremely suffocating sensation. A battle agains surrender inside her own mind. 

I am not sure how to interpret her relationship with Nick. It might be just another person trying to manipulate her, or it may be a relationship like that of _"1984"_ where sex is a political act, a small rebellion. Perhaps it's the author saying that feeling human is a way to rebel against the oppression. 

Personally I found the ending to be a little out of place. At no other point are we lead to believe there's anything to be optimistic about. The world is crude and cruel except at the end. It was a bit odd to me that the ending provided a false sense of hope and concluded with a satisfied ending.

Despite this, The Handmaiden's Tale is an incredible book that I would recommend to anyone who is looking to understand the true challenges that women face in today's world. It is a powerful reminder of why we need to fight for equality, and why it is so important to read and listen to the stories of women everywhere.
